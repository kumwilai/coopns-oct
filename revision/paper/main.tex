\title{Backbone-Modular Neuro-Symbolic Post-Correction for OCT Denoising via Explicit Predicate Maps and Stable Fuzzy Allocation}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)\authorrefmark{1}}
\affil{\authorrefmark{1} Anonymous Affiliation}
\corresp{Correspondence withheld for double blind review.}

\begin{abstract}
Denoisers for optical coherence tomography are trained to reduce a pixel
error, so they can raise fidelity while lowering the properties a clinician reads, namely layer
contrast, boundary definition and the visibility of weak structures. This paper treats the repair of
those properties as a separate problem. A frozen denoiser is kept unchanged and a small policy is
attached to it. The policy measures six named clinical properties in closed form, converts them into
a per pixel decision through five explicit rules, and limits how far the image may move. We prove
that the decision layer responds to a change in evidence by at most an exact constant below
\num{0.75}, and we measure the realised response against that constant on real images. We give three
conditions under which the correction cannot lower contrast to noise ratio, and report how often they
hold. The policy adds \num{168630} shared trainable parameters plus a small backbone specific head to a
backbone of about seven million. On the 173 held out test
images, with the same rules and the same fixed weights for every backbone, the correction improves
21 of the 24 backbone and measure pairs by more than their spread across three seeds, leaves two
within that spread, and lowers one, boundary sharpness on DnCNN by 1.4 percent from a starting value
already above the clean reference, at a fidelity cost of 0.17 to 0.98 dB. No clinical measure falls on three of the four backbones. On two other scanners with no adaptation
of any kind NAFNet and SwinIR improve every measure, while DnCNN and KBNet each lose the same two
measures on both scanners, 40 of 48 pairs in all.
\end{abstract}
