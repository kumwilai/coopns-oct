\title{Backbone-Modular Neuro-Symbolic Post-Correction for OCT Denoising via Explicit Predicate Maps and Stable Fuzzy Allocation}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)\authorrefmark{1}}
\affil{\authorrefmark{1} Anonymous Affiliation}
\corresp{Correspondence withheld for double blind review.}

\begin{abstract}
Optical coherence tomography is read through the contrast between retinal layers and the sharpness of
their boundaries. A denoiser is trained on a pixel error instead, so it can raise fidelity and still
lose them. Nothing sits between the denoiser and the reader to say what a
good scan looks like and repair where one falls short. We make that repair a problem of its
own, solved after denoising with the denoiser frozen. Four things are new. Six clinical properties in closed
form read the noisy input and the denoised output alone, each returning a score and a map of where it
fails, so they run on any scanner. Five rules in fuzzy logic turn those maps into a per-pixel
allocation whose response to a shift in evidence we bound by an exact, attained constant. A bounded proposal is admitted according to how many of three constraints hold, we prove when
contrast cannot fall, and the gain the rules allocate traces to a named property and place. One architecture, one set of rules and one set of budgets serve four denoisers of
different families, only the head that reads features resized, though each is fitted separately. All six measures rise on two of the four, five of six on the others,
contrast by 3.5 to 11.3 percent, speckle suppression by 23.7 to 57.1 and signal to noise by 8.0 to
23.0, at 0.17 to 0.98 dB of fidelity. Carried unchanged to two other datasets, 40 of 48 improve.
\end{abstract}
