\title{Backbone-Modular Neuro-Symbolic Post-Correction for OCT Denoising via Explicit Predicate Maps and Stable Fuzzy Allocation}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)\authorrefmark{1}}
\affil{\authorrefmark{1} Anonymous Affiliation}
\corresp{Correspondence withheld for double blind review.}

\begin{abstract}
Denoisers for optical coherence tomography are trained to reduce a pixel error, so they can raise
fidelity while lowering what a clinician reads, the contrast between layers and the sharpness of
their boundaries. We repair those properties separately. A small wrapper on the frozen denoiser
measures six named clinical properties from the noisy input and the denoised output alone, needing no
clean image at test time, turns them into a per-pixel decision through five rules, and bounds the
change. We prove that the decision moves by at most an exact constant below \num{0.75}
when the evidence changes, measure the realised response, and give three conditions under which the
correction cannot lower contrast to noise ratio. Only the head reading backbone features changes with it. The shared part is \num{168630} parameters and that head adds \num{4659}
to \num{234273} more, so the wrapper is 2.3 to 5.8 percent of a seven million parameter denoiser and
the same rules and weights serve four backbones. On 173 test images it improves 21 of 24 backbone and measure
pairs beyond their seed spread and lowers one, costing 0.17 to 0.98 dB. Without adaptation 40 of 48
pairs improve on two other datasets.
\end{abstract}
