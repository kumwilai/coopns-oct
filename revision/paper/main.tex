\title{Backbone-Modular Neuro-Symbolic Post-Correction for OCT Denoising via Explicit Predicate Maps and Stable Fuzzy Allocation}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)\authorrefmark{1}}
\affil{\authorrefmark{1} Anonymous Affiliation}
\corresp{Correspondence withheld for double blind review.}

\begin{abstract}
Denoisers for optical coherence tomography are trained to reduce a pixel error, so they can raise
fidelity while lowering what a clinician actually reads, the contrast between layers, the definition
of their boundaries and the visibility of weak structure. This paper treats the repair of those
properties as a separate problem. The denoiser is frozen and a small wrapper is attached to it. The
wrapper measures six named clinical properties from the noisy input and the denoised output alone, so
it needs no clean image at test time, turns them into a per pixel decision through five explicit
rules, and limits how far the image may move. We prove that the decision responds to a change in
evidence by at most an exact constant below \num{0.75} and measure the realised response on real
images, and we give three conditions under which the correction cannot lower contrast to noise ratio
and report how often they hold. Only the head that reads backbone features changes with the backbone.
The shared part is \num{168630} trainable parameters, about 2.4 percent of a seven million parameter
denoiser, and the same rules, budgets and loss weights serve four backbone families. On 173 held out
test images the correction improves 21 of the 24 backbone and measure pairs by more than their spread
across three seeds, leaves two within it, and lowers one, boundary sharpness on DnCNN by 1.4 percent
from a value already above the clean reference, at a fidelity cost of 0.17 to 0.98 dB. On two other
scanners with no adaptation of any kind 40 of 48 pairs improve, every one of them for NAFNet and
SwinIR.
\end{abstract}
