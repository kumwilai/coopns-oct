\section{Training procedure and the full list of constants}
\label{sec:supp_training}

\subsection{Algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:supp_training} states the procedure. The backbone is frozen throughout and
receives no gradient. The objective is evaluated on the candidate, because the acceptance rule is
skipped while the model is in training mode, so Equation~(\EqObjective) of the main paper is
optimized at $\mathbf{q}$ and not at $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$. The first stage activates only the clinical terms, so the correctors learn a
useful direction. The second stage activates the fidelity term, so they learn how far to go.

\subsection{Schedule and optimizer settings}
We use AdamW with decoupled weight decay of $10^{-4}$, random crops of $96 \times 96$ pixels, and a
batch of sixteen crops, reduced to eight for DnCNN and SwinIR, the two with the largest memory footprint.
Three learning rates are used, a lower one for the corrector networks and higher ones for the
strength predictor and the rule layer, because the last two have far fewer parameters and therefore
need larger steps to move at all. The switch from the clinical only stage to the stage that also
includes fidelity happens at the midpoint of the schedule, matching the epoch check in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:supp_training}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Training the wrapper.}
\label{alg:supp_training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require frozen backbone $f_\theta$, paired training set, number of epochs $E$
\State initialize the wrapper parameters, set each property threshold to its literature value
\For{$e = 1$ to $E$}
  \For{each batch of crops}
    \State compute the backbone output, with no gradient to $\theta$
    \State compute the cooperation map from the backbone features
    \State compute the six property scores and failure maps
    \State compute the gain, edge and strength proposals
    \State compute the five rule truth values
    \State compute the allocation
    \State compose the candidate $\mathbf{q}$, then evaluate the objective on it
    \State \Comment{the acceptance rule of the main paper does not run here}
    \State update the wrapper parameters with AdamW
  \EndFor
  \If{$e$ reaches the midpoint} activate the fidelity term \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{The nine quantities of the clinical term}
\label{sec:supp_clinical_terms}
Equation~(\EqLossClin) of the main paper names nine quantities and their weights. Their definitions
are here, so the term can be rebuilt. Write $\sigma_7$ for the local standard deviation over a
$7 \times 7$ window, $\nabla_z$ and $\nabla_x$ for the vertical and horizontal Sobel responses,
$\nabla^2$ for the Laplacian, $e = \sqrt{\nabla_x^2 + \nabla_z^2 + \varepsilon}$ for the edge
magnitude, and $\langle \cdot \rangle$ for the mean over every pixel of the batch. All nine are
scalars per batch, and the first six are measured against the clean reference $\mathbf{x}$.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item Local contrast, $\langle \sigma_7(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \rangle / \langle \sigma_7(\mathbf{x}) \rangle$.
\item Boundary gradient, $\langle |\nabla_z \hat{\mathbf{x}}| \rangle / \langle |\nabla_z \mathbf{x}| \rangle$.
\item Texture, $\langle |\nabla^2 \hat{\mathbf{x}}| \rangle / \langle |\nabla^2 \mathbf{x}| \rangle$.
\item EPI, the Pearson correlation between $e(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ and $e(\mathbf{x})$, taken per image
over all pixels and then averaged over the batch.
\item CNR, $(\mu_U - \mu_R) / \sigma_R$ on the corrected image, with $U$ the upper half and $R$ the
lower quarter of the frame.
\item BS, $\max |\nabla_z \hat{\mathbf{x}}| / \max |\nabla_z \mathbf{x}|$, clipped to $[0,10]$, then
divided by the same ratio for the backbone.
\item ENL, $(\mu_R / \sigma_R)^2$, and SNR, $\mu_U / \sigma_R$, each as the exponential of the mean
log ratio to the backbone value over the batch.
\item The squared error ratio, $\lVert \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{x} \rVert_2^2 / \lVert \hat{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x} \rVert_2^2$.
\end{itemize}
Every denominator is clamped away from zero at $10^{-6}$. The target $t_m$ of each is the same
quantity on the backbone output, held with no gradient and scaled by the factor $\kappa_m$ the main
paper lists, except ENL, SNR and the squared error ratio, whose targets are the fixed values 1.10,
1.15 and 0.87 rather than backbone multiples.

\subsection{The selection rule in full}
\label{sec:supp_selection}
The main paper says a setting is admissible only at its worst seed and that the winner has the
largest lower confidence bound on its weakest clinical measure. Both are stated here exactly, since
they decide the reported operating point.

For a setting with $n$ seeds, let $m_s = \min_i \Delta_i^{(s)}$ be the weakest of the six clinical
changes at seed $s$. The lower confidence bound is the one sided quantity
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{LCB} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_s m_s \;-\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\,\mathrm{sd}(m_1,\ldots,m_n),
\end{equation}
which is the mean of the worst measure less one standard error of that mean, and is the mean itself
when $n = 1$. A setting is admissible at a level $(c, f)$ when its fidelity loss at its worst seed is
at most $c$ decibels and its weakest clinical change at its worst seed exceeds $f$ percent. The four
levels are applied in order, strict $(0.8, 0.5)$, standard $(1.0, 0.2)$, positive $(1.0, 0.0)$ and
unbounded $(\infty, 0.0)$. The first level that admits any setting decides the choice, and the level
used is recorded, so a backbone that needed a looser condition is visible rather than hidden. Among
the admissible settings the winner is the largest LCB, with the mean fidelity and then the mean over
the six measures breaking ties. If no level admits any setting the script reports the setting failing
on the fewest measures and labels the outcome as no selection.

That last branch is the one two backbones took. NAFNet and SwinIR were admitted at the strict level,
with lower bounds of $+1.15$ and $+1.77$. No setting in the grid cleared any of the four levels for
DnCNN or KBNet, whose best lower bounds were $-1.95$ and $-0.05$, so for those two the reported
setting is the least bad of the grid and not a setting that met a stated bound. They are also the two
backbones with a clinical measure that falls on test, which is consistent with a grid that had
nothing admissible to offer. Seeds 0, 1 and 2 are used throughout, and the seed 0 checkpoint of the
selected setting is the one every figure draws.

\subsection{Constants that carry a design decision}
\label{sec:supp_constants_design}
Table~\ref{tab:constants} lists the constants that carry a design decision, where each acts, and how
it was chosen. A constant marked \emph{measured} was set from a property of the data. A constant
marked \emph{selected} was chosen on the validation split and never on the test split. A constant
marked \emph{fixed} is a structural choice that was not tuned.

The networks use the default initialization of their layers. Two exceptions are deliberate. The bias
of the brightness head starts at a small positive value so that the multiplicative branch begins from
a mild brightening rather than from zero, which avoids a flat region of the loss at the start of
training. The threshold of each property is declared as a trainable parameter and set to the value reported in
the clinical literature for that property. It receives no gradient under the objective of the main
paper, and in every selected checkpoint all six sit at their literature values to four decimals, so
they are fixed in effect and the main paper reports them as fixed.

\subsection{What the paired data is used for}
Training uses paired data. The clean reference enters the fidelity term, the clinical term and the
supervision of the edge branch. Testing uses no clean image anywhere in the correction path. The six
properties are computed from the noisy input and the backbone output only, which is what allows them
to run on a scanner the method has never seen. The clean reference is used to score results, as in
any denoising study, and never to produce them.
