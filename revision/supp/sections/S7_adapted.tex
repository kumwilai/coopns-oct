\section{The adapted transfer protocol of the original submission, a test selected diagnostic}
\label{sec:supp_adapted}

\noindent\textbf{These numbers are not an estimate of generalization.} Each fold keeps the epoch that
scores best on the subject it then reports, so they are selected on the data they describe. They are
here as a record of what the original submission did and of the gap between the two protocols, and
the main paper reports the frozen protocol throughout.

The cross dataset numbers of the original submission were not produced by the no adaptation
protocol the text described. The script that produced them resumed the corrector trained on PKU37
and trained it further on the target dataset in a leave-one-subject-out design. We rerun that
protocol here with the selected NAFNet checkpoint so that the gap between the two protocols can be
read directly, and we describe it exactly.

\subsection{Protocol}
Each fold holds out one subject, seventeen of Duke2013 or fifteen of Duke17 subjects form the
training set, and the held-out subject serves as both the validation subject and the reported one. Training resumes
from the PKU37 checkpoint
of the tissue-bounded gate setting at seed 0 and runs for fifteen epochs with batch size two. The
backbone weights and its normalization statistics are frozen. The wrapper and the cooperation head
that reads the backbone features are trainable. The learning rates are $2{\times}10^{-5}$,
$1{\times}10^{-4}$ and $6{\times}10^{-5}$ for the corrector, potential and negotiator, five to ten
times below the PKU37 schedule of Table~\ref{tab:constants}, the fidelity term enters at epoch five,
and an L2 penalty of weight 0.1 holds every trainable weight near its PKU37 value, which is elastic
weight consolidation without the Fisher weighting. A background correction variance penalty of
weight 10 is also active. The checkpoint kept for each fold is the epoch with the highest validation
score, and the validation image of a fold is its held-out subject. The adapted numbers are therefore
selected on the subject they are evaluated on. That makes them optimistic rather than an upper bound
in any proved sense, and they should be read as a diagnostic of what the original submission did. The same was true of the original submission. We did not repair this by reselecting, since
no unbiased checkpoint rule can be recovered from folds that were already trained this way.

Two patterns appear on both scanners, and neither can be read as an effect of adaptation for the
reason given above. Contrast is higher, by 3.2 points on Duke17 and 8.1 on Duke2013, on every
subject. Fidelity is lower than the frozen model by a
further 0.06 dB on Duke17 and 0.10 dB on Duke2013, with one and two subjects losing more than 1 dB,
and the signal to noise ratio is 2.5 and 14.6 percentage points lower. The mean absolute correction rises from
0.0104 to 0.0121 on Duke17 and from 0.0118 to 0.0164 on Duke2013. The other measures do not agree
between the two sets. On Duke17 the tissue contrast index reads $+2.7$ against $+4.0$ percent
while the speckle measure is higher, and no measure is below zero. On Duke2013 the tissue contrast
index, edge preservation and boundary sharpness are all higher, the speckle measure moves one way on
eight subjects and the other on ten, and the adapted signal to noise ratio reads $-5.29$ percent
against $+9.29$ for the frozen model, a difference of 14.58 percentage points in the means, lower on
fifteen of the eighteen subjects. The mean and that count are separate statistics, and neither says
the decline is the same on every subject. We attach no significance language to any of this, for the
reason below.

We draw no conclusion about what adaptation achieves. These numbers are kept only to document the
procedure the original submission used. Every checkpoint was chosen on the subject it is then scored
on, and the comparison is not seed matched, so the differences above are not
unbiased evidence that adaptation helps or hurts any measure. They cannot support a confirmatory
claim in either direction. The main conclusions of this paper rest on the frozen transfer of Table 5
of the main paper, which gives six positive measures on both sets with nothing fitted on the target
data.
