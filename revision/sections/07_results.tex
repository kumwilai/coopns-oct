\section{Tests and Results}
\label{sec:tests}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsubsection{Data}
Three retinal OCT datasets from three different sources are used, listed in
Table~\ref{tab:datasets}. Paired data is expensive in OCT because a clean reference requires many
repeated acquisitions under steady fixation~\cite{geng2022triplet}, so these are among the largest
public paired sets available. PKU37~\cite{geng2022triplet} provides 37 cross sections from a
spectral domain scanner with references averaged over 50 frames. We split it by clean image identity
into 27 for training, 5 for validation and 5 for testing, which yields 1388, 173 and 173 noisy and
clean pairs. Splitting by identity rather than by pair is what prevents a noisy realisation of a
training image from appearing in the test set. Duke17~\cite{fang2012sparsity} provides 16 paired B
scans from a clinical scanner at a different institution. Duke2013~\cite{fang2013fast} provides 18
volumes with controlled degradation.

\subsubsection{The transfer protocol, stated exactly}
\label{sec:protocol}
This point was ambiguous in the previous version of this paper and we state it precisely here.

Every model is trained once on the PKU37 training split. The Duke17 and Duke2013 results in
Table~\ref{tab:transfer} are produced by applying that model directly to the external images. No
parameter is updated on Duke data. No threshold is recalibrated on Duke data. No statistic of the
Duke images is used to select anything. The only quantity computed per image is the intensity
percentile inside the gate of Equation~\eqref{eq:gate}, which is part of the method itself and is
computed identically on PKU37.

For completeness we also report the alternative protocol, in which the model is allowed a short
adaptation on the other images of the same external dataset before being applied to a held out image.
Reporting both separates what transfers without cost from what has to be paid for, and
Section~\ref{sec:results_ood} gives the gap between them.

\subsubsection{Measures}
Seven quantities are reported. Peak signal to noise ratio measures fidelity against the clean
reference. The other six are clinical and are reported as a percentage change from the backbone
output to the corrected output. Contrast to noise ratio measures the separation of tissue from
background. Tissue contrast index measures dynamic range inside tissue. Edge preservation index
measures agreement of the edge map with the reference. Boundary sharpness measures the gradient
across layer boundaries. Equivalent number of looks measures speckle suppression. Signal to noise
ratio measures signal strength against the noise floor. Every measure is computed per image and then
averaged, never pooled across a batch, because pooling inflates the result.

\subsubsection{Cost}
The wrapper adds \num{168630} trainable parameters to a frozen backbone of about seven million,
which is an increase of about 2.4 percent, summarised at the foot of Table~\ref{tab:datasets}.

\subsection{Contribution of each clinical property}
\label{sec:predicate_ablation}
Reviewer feedback asked whether all six properties are needed. We remove one property at a time at
test time, which sets its score to the satisfied value and its failure map to zero, and re evaluate
the whole test set. Fig.~\ref{fig:studies} reports the result. TBDRESULTS

\subsection{Sensitivity to the rule constants}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
The rule layer contains a base level, five rule weights and five budgets. We vary each in turn and
re evaluate. Fig.~\ref{fig:studies} reports the result. We also replace the Lukasiewicz
conjunction by the product and by the minimum, which are the two other standard choices, to test
whether the specific logic matters. TBDRESULTS

\subsection{Comparison at matched complexity}
\label{sec:matched}
A reviewer asked for a comparison against methods of similar complexity built with the same design
approach. We therefore compare against three alternatives that all act on the same frozen backbone
output. The first is a plain convolutional post corrector with the same parameter budget, trained
with the same objective, with no properties, no rules and no safety layer. The second and third are
classical post processing operators, namely unsharp masking and contrast limited adaptive histogram
equalisation, tuned on the validation split. The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the result. TBDRESULTS

\subsection{Does the confidence map predict error}
\label{sec:confidence_check}
A reviewer observed that nothing in the objective forces the confidence map to behave like a
confidence. We test it directly. For every test image we compare the map against the absolute error
of the backbone output with respect to the clean reference. The rank correlation, the sparsification error and the expected calibration error after a single affine
fit. TBDRESULTS

\subsection{Do the theoretical conditions hold on real data}
\label{sec:theory_check}
This subsection connects Section~\ref{sec:stability} to measurements, which is what a reviewer asked
for. Three things are checked. For the stability result we perturb the rule truth values by a
controlled amount and measure the ratio in Equation~\eqref{eq:stability_def}, then compare it with
the constant of Equation~\eqref{eq:lipschitz}. For the contrast result we measure the tissue gain,
the background leakage and the ratio of background standard deviations on every image, and report how
often the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cnr} hold. For the safety layer we report how often each of
the three constraints fails and which one is binding. TBDRESULTS

\subsection{Safety, and whether the correction invents structure}
\label{sec:safety_check}
A correction that raises contrast could in principle sharpen a boundary that is not there, or erase a
faint one that is. Because PKU37 provides a clean reference we can measure both directly. We report
the rate at which an edge appears in the corrected image where the reference has none, the retention
of weak structures whose contrast in the reference is in the lowest decile, the behaviour inside dark
fluid like regions enclosed by tissue, and the worst case per image change across the whole test set.
Fig.~\ref{fig:studies} reports the result. TBDRESULTS

We state one limitation plainly. No clinician read these images. The analysis above is quantitative
and uses the averaged reference as the standard of truth. A reader study is the correct next step and
is outside the scope of this revision.

\subsection{Cost of moving to a new backbone}
\label{sec:transfer_cost}
Only the confidence head depends on the backbone, and adapting it is additional training, so its cost
should be stated rather than assumed away. For each of the four backbones we report the size of the head, the data used to fit it, the number of epochs, the wall clock time and the spread across repeated runs. TBDRESULTS
