\section{Tests and Results}
\label{sec:tests}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\emph{Data.} PKU37~\cite{geng2022triplet} provides 37 cross sections of $640 \times 640$ pixels from a
spectral domain scanner, references averaged over 50 frames, split by clean image identity into 27, 5
and 5 to give 1388, 173 and 173 pairs. It is the only training domain.
Duke17~\cite{fang2012sparsity} provides 17 paired B-scans of $970 \times 520$ from another
institution, of which we use the 16 the released loader reads, and Duke2013~\cite{fang2013fast} the
18 volumes of $900 \times 450$ of its test split. Both Duke sets come from the same manufacturer and the 2013 evaluation reuses some 2012 material, so
we call them two external datasets rather than two independent scanners.

\emph{Transfer protocol.} Every model is trained once on PKU37 and applied to the Duke sets directly,
with nothing fitted, recalibrated or selected on Duke data beyond the per image quantity the gate of
Equation~\eqref{eq:gate} computes by the same rule as on PKU37. The original submission instead
adapted the wrapper for fifteen further epochs on the other subjects of the target set, leave one
subject out. Each of its folds keeps the epoch that scores best on the subject it then reports, so
those numbers are selected on the data they describe. We rerun the protocol for the record and give
it in the supplementary file as a historical diagnostic.

\emph{Measures.} Peak signal to noise ratio against the clean reference measures fidelity. Six
clinical measures, given as percentage change from backbone to corrected output, are the contrast to
noise ratio of tissue against background, the tissue contrast index for dynamic range in tissue, the
edge preservation index for agreement with the reference edge map, boundary sharpness for the
gradient across layer boundaries, the equivalent number of looks for speckle suppression, and the
signal to noise ratio against the noise floor. Each is computed per image then averaged, never
pooled, since pooling inflates the result. The 173 pairs come from five clean image identities, so
every count below describes repeated acquisitions of five anatomies and not a population.

\subsection{In distribution results}
\label{sec:results_id}
Table~\ref{tab:pku37} reports the PKU37 test set, Fig.~\ref{fig:subjective_all} the corrected scans
at full resolution and Fig.~\ref{fig:subjective_pku37} the band where the correction is largest. The
wrapper raises contrast on every backbone, by 3.5 to 11.3 percent, and the speckle measure by 23.7 to
57.1 percent, for 0.17 dB of fidelity at best and under one decibel at worst. Twenty one of the 24
backbone by measure pairs improve by more than one seed spread, one improves inside it, one is
unchanged and one falls, the boundary sharpness of DnCNN. The gain holds image by image and not only
in the mean. Contrast rose on all but twelve of the 2076 image and seed combinations, none of the
twelve by as much as half a percent, and it is largest where the backbone leaves most to repair.

\subsection{Transfer to other datasets}
\label{sec:results_ood}
Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot} carries the same four models to Duke17 and Duke2013 with nothing refitted.
All six measures stay positive on both sets for NAFNet and SwinIR at under 0.35 dB, and 40 of the 48
backbone by measure by set pairs are positive. The eight exceptions are the tissue contrast index and
the boundary sharpness of DnCNN and the edge preservation and the signal to noise ratio of KBNet.
Contrast rises on every backbone and both sets.

\subsection{Which properties and which constants matter}
\label{sec:predicate_ablation}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
One property at a time is switched off at test time, by setting its score to the satisfied value and
its failure map to zero, and the test set is re evaluated. Only $P_5$, $P_1$ and $P_2$ move any
measure by more than a quarter point, costing 3.19, 1.72 and 0.74 points of the speckle gain, while
the other three change nothing by more than 0.23. We keep all six, because they are the vocabulary of
the rule layer and of the explanation shown to the reader, and one that rarely fires here can fire on
another scanner.

We then vary the base level and the two rule weights that carry the allocation, and replace the
Lukasiewicz conjunction by the product and by the minimum. The budgets are tied by the identity of
Section~\ref{sec:allocation_defect} and stay fixed. None of the fourteen variations turns a measure
negative, contrast staying between 3.5 and 4.5 percent and fidelity between 0.16 and 0.23 dB. Only
the base level has a visible effect, lowering it raising contrast and costing fidelity together,
while the two weights move the result by under a fifth of a point. The result therefore rests on the
shape of the rule layer and not on the particular conjunction.

\subsection{What each component contributes, and how it compares}
\label{sec:component_ablation}
\label{sec:matched}
Table~\ref{tab:components} removes one component at a time from the selected NAFNet model. Replacing
the rule layer by a uniform allocation cuts the contrast gain from 3.7 to 0.8 percent and the tissue
contrast index from 2.4 to 0.4 while doubling the fidelity cost, the clearest evidence that the
allocation and not the capacity of the correctors produces the clinical gain. Removing the edge
branch turns both negative, removing the cooperation map costs about a third of the contrast gain,
and removing background smoothing leaves contrast and the two edge measures almost intact while the
speckle measure collapses from 23.3 to 5.8 percent.

The lower block spends a similar budget three other ways on the same backbone output. The first is
the same architecture, objective and ten epochs with the rule layer replaced by a uniform allocation
throughout training rather than only at test time. It was fitted before the gate search, so it
carries the percentile gate and not the tissue-bounded gate NAFNet was selected with, which makes it
matched in architecture and not in configuration. It reaches a larger contrast gain and a much larger
tissue contrast index for 0.42 dB against 0.17 and is worse on four of the six measures, so the two
are not ordered. What the full method buys with its fidelity is the account of why each change was
made. The other two are classical operators tuned on the validation split. Both reach a tissue
contrast index far above anything the wrapper does and both fail elsewhere, lowering edge
preservation by about 3.5 percent and the speckle measure by about 36 percent while raising boundary
sharpness by about 25 percent by sharpening noise. Adaptive equalisation also costs 4.4 dB.

\subsection{Does the cooperation map predict error}
\label{sec:confidence_check}
Called a confidence map in the original submission, this component was tested against the absolute
error of the backbone output over \NImages{} images, and it does not predict that error. The quantity
tested is the demand map $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{c}$, high where the wrapper is told to
act, so a demand map tracking backbone error would give a positive rank correlation. The measured
value is \CalibRho{}, ordered the wrong way rather than merely uninformative, and removing the pixels
it ranks least reliable raises the remaining error to 1.88 times the starting value where an oracle
ranking reaches 0.07. The map is nevertheless useful, since replacing it by a constant lowers the
contrast gain from $+3.71$ to $+2.51$ percent and worsens five of the six measures. Nothing in the
objective shows the head the error of the backbone, so we call it a cooperation map and not a
confidence.

\subsection{Do the theoretical conditions hold on real data}
\label{sec:theory_check}
We perturb the rule truth values and compare the ratio of
Equation~\eqref{eq:stability_def} with the constant of Equation~\eqref{eq:lipschitz}, and we measure
the tissue gain, the background leakage and the ratio of background standard deviations against the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cnr}. The bound holds with room to spare, \LipTheory{} against a
largest measured ratio of \LipMeasured{} over \NImages{} images. The constant is exact for the
allocation as a function of the rule truth values, so the gap is not slack in the proof. It says the
perturbations a real image produces never reach the worst case the constant allows. The allocation
map spans \AllocMin{} to \AllocMax{} with mean \AllocMean{}, and \AllocClamp{} percent of pixels sit
at either end of its clamp, direct evidence that the rule layer does work after the repair of
Section~\ref{sec:allocation_defect}.

The margin condition holds on \CondMargin{} of \NImages{} images and the ratio condition on
\CondSigma{}, but both together on only \CondBoth{}, and the theorem needs both. Where a hypothesis
fails the theorem predicts nothing, and the measured contrast change there does not differ
systematically in sign, so the conditions are sufficient and not necessary. Both leakage terms sit
far below the mean absolute change over the image, \GlobalChange{}, at \EtaT{} in tissue and \EtaB{}
in background, and the per image sign is the direction the decomposition assumes on \EtaTPos{} and
\EtaBNeg{} of \NImages{} images.

\subsection{Safety, and whether the correction invents structure}
\label{sec:safety_check}
A correction that raises contrast could sharpen a boundary that is not there or erase a faint one
that is, and the clean reference lets us measure both. Two measures move against it. An edge appears
where the reference has none on \EdgeInvCorr{} percent of pixels after correction against
\EdgeInvBack{} percent for the backbone, a rise on \EdgeInvUp{} of \NImages{} images, and the
retention of structures in the lowest decile of reference contrast falls from \WeakRetBack{} to
\WeakRetCorr{} percent, on \WeakRetDown{}. Both are significant under a two sided Wilcoxon signed
rank test on the paired per image values, at $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-12}$ and
$p = 6.2 \times 10^{-30}$. Neither is zero by construction, since the clamp bounds how far a pixel
may move and not how many cross an edge threshold, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} lists both. What
separates the wrapper from a sharpening filter is Table~\ref{tab:components}, where the classical
sharpeners lose edge preservation and the speckle measure while it gains on both.

Three checks come out in its favour. The two layer boundaries barely move, the inner limiting
membrane shift falling from \IlmShiftBack{} to \IlmShiftCorr{} pixels and the retinal pigment
epithelium shift changing by less than a signed rank test can separate from zero. Dark fluid like
regions occupy \DarkFrac{} percent of the image with a mean absolute change of \DarkChange{} inside
them against \GlobalChange{} overall, the largest change anywhere being \GlobalMax{}, so the
correction is quieter where a dark region carries the finding. Boundary sharpness is not worsened on
\BoundOK{} of \NImages{} images. Of the three constraints of Section~\ref{sec:safety}, energy holds
on \ConEnergy{} and bounded change on \ConLip{}, and only the no sacrifice constraint ever fails, on
\NImages{} minus \ConPareto{}. None falls below two, so the candidate is returned in full everywhere
and the layer never acted on this test set.

\subsection{Cost of moving to a new backbone}
\label{sec:transfer_cost}
A new backbone means refitting the whole wrapper, not only its head. The trainable count is
\num{168630} shared parameters plus a head whose width follows the feature tensor it reads, from
\num{4659} for KBNet to \num{234273} for DnCNN, where the first convolution is wide. Against a frozen
backbone of about seven million that is 2.3 to 5.8 percent, so the shared part is constant and the
cost of attaching it follows how wide a tensor the backbone exposes. Every backbone is fitted on the
same 1388 pairs for the same ten epochs, so only the wall clock and the seed spread vary. One seed
takes 2.0 minutes for NAFNet and 8.3 for SwinIR on a single RTX 5070 Ti, following the cost of one
backbone forward pass, and the seed spread of the fidelity cost runs from 0.02 dB for NAFNet to 0.21
for DnCNN. The backbone with the widest spread is the only one whose clinical measure falls.
