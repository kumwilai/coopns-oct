\section{Training, Tests and Results}
\label{sec:tests}
\label{sec:training}

\subsection{The six loss terms}
\label{sec:loss_terms}
Let $\mathbf{x}$ be the clean reference, $\mathbf{b}$ the backbone output and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ the
wrapper output. The objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} has the following terms.

\textbf{Fidelity.} Let $D = \mathrm{PSNR}(\mathbf{b}) - \mathrm{PSNR}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ be the drop
against the backbone in decibels and $D_s$ the corresponding drop in the structural similarity
index~\cite{wang2004image}, both taken against the clean reference $\mathbf{x}$. The symbol is $D$, since
Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha} already uses $\Delta$ for the gain map. Neither is charged until it exceeds
a dead zone,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fid}} = 5\,[D - d]_+ + 15\,[D - d - 0.4]_+^2
 + 20\,[D_s - 0.005]_+ ,
\label{eq:loss_fid}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_+$ is the positive part. The term is flat while the drop stays below $d$ decibels,
linear for the next four tenths and quadratic beyond, so a small loss of fidelity is free and a large
one expensive. The dead zone $d$ is one of the two quantities selected per backbone in
Section~\ref{sec:weight_selection}.

\textbf{Clinical improvement.} This term charges the wrapper only when a measure falls short of a
target, so a gain in one cannot be bought by a collapse in another. Let $q_m$ be quantity $m$ on the
wrapper output and $t_m = \kappa_m q_m(\mathbf{b})$ its target, the backbone value scaled by a fixed
factor. Writing $\varepsilon_m = t_m - q_m$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clin}} = \frac{1}{\sum_m w_m} \sum_{m \in M} w_m
 \Big( 3[\varepsilon_m]_+ + [\varepsilon_m]_+^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} s_m \varepsilon_m^2 \Big).
\label{eq:loss_clin}
\end{equation}
The set $M$ holds nine quantities, the three preservation scores of local contrast, boundary gradient
and texture, then EPI, CNR, BS, ENL, SNR and the ratio of the backbone squared error to the wrapper
squared error, each defined in the supplementary file with its window, mask and reduction. Their
weights $w_m$ are 1, 5, 1, 15, 40, 5, 8, 40 and 10 and their factors $\kappa_m$ are 1.12, 1.12, 1.00,
1.05, 1.20, 1.12, 1.10, 1.15 and 0.87, the last allowing about six tenths of a decibel. The indicator
$s_m$ is one for ENL, SNR and the squared error ratio, a symmetric pull holding those three near
target. None of the nine is used at test time.

\textbf{Preservation scores.} This term acts on four scores $g_j$ separate from the six properties of
Table~\ref{tab:predicates}, comparing corrected against backbone on local contrast, the boundary
gradient, the local variance carrying texture and the edge magnitude. With
$\delta_j = g_j(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - g_j(\mathbf{b})$, the backbone value fixed,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} = \sum_{j=1}^{4} v_j \Big( 3[-\delta_j]_+ - 2.5[\delta_j]_+
 + 2\big[\tfrac{1}{2} - g_j(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\big]_+ \Big) ,
\label{eq:loss_pred}
\end{equation}
where $v_j$ is 3, 2, 2 and 1 in that order. A drop costs more than a rise earns, and any score below
one half is charged again. The thresholds $\tau_i$ play no part here. They enter
Equation~\eqref{eq:soft_failure}, evaluated in a module whose output reaches the objective only
through detached quantities, so no gradient path reaches $\tau_i$ and all six hold their
Table~\ref{tab:predicates} values throughout training. This is not true of the rule layer
as a whole. The base level $\beta$ and the rule weights $\theta_k$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:allocation} are differentiable and do receive gradients, which
Section~\ref{sec:gradient_audit} measures parameter by parameter.

\textbf{Cooperation.} The wrapper should act where the backbone leaves room and stay quiet elsewhere.
We take the Pearson correlation $\rho$ between the demand map $\mathbf{u}$ and $\boldsymbol{\pi}$, the
summed proposal strength the rule layer reads, and penalize disagreement,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coop}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\big(1 - \rho(\mathbf{u}, \boldsymbol{\pi})\big).
\label{eq:loss_coop}
\end{equation}
Each map is first rescaled to $[0,1]$ by its own minimum and maximum, so the term is invariant to a
positive affine change of either but not to a negative one, which reverses the sign. The correlation
is undefined for a constant map, where the implementation returns a zero loss, the one place this
term is not differentiable.

\textbf{Edge supervision.} The additive branch is trained to reproduce the gradient that the backbone
lost, measured with the Sobel operator $\nabla$ and weighted toward the places where the clean
reference has an edge. Let $\mathbf{w}$ be the clean Sobel magnitude divided by its mean and clipped
at ten, $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ its square normalized again to unit mean, and $\bar{\mathbf{g}}$ a Sobel
magnitude divided by its own maximum. With $\delta = [1.05\,\mathrm{EPI}(\mathbf{b}) -
\mathrm{EPI}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})]_+$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{edge}} = \;&10\,\big\lVert \sqrt{\mathbf{w}} \odot
  (\nabla\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \nabla\mathbf{x}) \big\rVert_2^2 \\
 &+ 10\,\big\lVert \sqrt{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}} \odot
  (\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x})) \big\rVert_2^2
 + 45\,(3\delta + \delta^2).
\end{split}
\label{eq:loss_edge}
\end{equation}
The first part is the gradient the correction must restore, the second compares normalized edge
magnitude, and the third refuses an edge preservation index below five percent above the backbone.

\textbf{Bounds and background protection.} The last term keeps the change small, smooth and
confined. Let $g$ be the mean absolute change over the upper half of the
image, named $g$ because Equation~\eqref{eq:allocation} already uses $a$, let $e$ be the mean
absolute output of the edge branch, and $\mathbf{m}$ a soft mask below the fifteenth percentile of
the backbone output. Here $\sigma_R$ and $\mu_U$ are taken over the lower quarter and the upper half
of the frame, fixed bands and not the anatomical sets $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cnr},
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}} = \;&\min\big(40[0.002 - g]_+,\, 5\big) \\
 &+ \min\big(200[g - 0.006]_+^2,\, 5\big) \\
 &+ 2\,\overline{\lVert \nabla \boldsymbol{\alpha} \rVert}
 + 30[e - 0.06]_+^2 \\
 &+ 80\big[\sigma_R(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - 0.75\,\sigma_R(\mathbf{b})\big]_+ \\
 &+ 100\Big[\tfrac{\sigma_R(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\sigma_R(\mathbf{b})} - 0.95\Big]_+ \\
 &+ 150\Big[0.99 - \tfrac{\mu_U(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\mu_U(\mathbf{b})}\Big]_+
 + 2\,\overline{\lvert \hat{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{b} \rvert \odot \mathbf{m}} ,
\end{split}
\label{eq:loss_reg}
\end{equation}
where a bar denotes the mean over pixels. Every squared norm in Equations~\eqref{eq:loss_edge}
and~\eqref{eq:loss_reg} is likewise a mean over pixels and over the batch, not a sum, so no term
changes scale with the crop size. The floor stops the wrapper
learning to do nothing and the ceiling stops it overshooting fidelity, the third keeps the admitted
gain $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha} smooth, not the allocation, so the correction
leaves no Sobel trace of its own, the fourth bounds the edge branch, and the last three hold the
background noise and the tissue brightness.

\subsection{Schedule, selection and constants}
\label{sec:paired_data}
\emph{Data and optimization schedule.} Training uses paired data, a noisy scan and a clean reference
averaged over repeated acquisitions. The reference enters the fidelity and clinical terms and
supervises the edge branch. Testing uses no clean image at any point, since the six properties read
the noisy input and the backbone output alone. The backbone is trained first with the squared error
and is then frozen. The wrapper follows in two stages, the clinical terms alone at first so the
correctors learn a useful direction, then the fidelity term from the midpoint so they learn how far
they may go. Both stages use AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$ on
$96 \times 96$ crops, in batches of sixteen and of eight for DnCNN and SwinIR, for ten epochs in all,
with base learning rates of $2 \times 10^{-4}$ for the correctors, $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for the
cooperation head and the strength predictor and $3 \times 10^{-4}$ for the rule layer. The
supplementary file restates the procedure as pseudocode.

The normalization statistics of $f$ are held fixed throughout. DnCNN is the one backbone carrying
batch normalization, and left in training mode its buffers move, so the reference every clinical
change of that backbone is measured against would drift while the correction was fitted to it.

\emph{How the operating point was chosen.} \label{sec:weight_selection}The terms of Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} carry fixed relative weights, the same for every backbone
and seed. The clinical term dominates at 6, the property term is 1, the cooperation term is 0.2, and
the fidelity term is 0 in the first stage and 1 in the second, which is what the two stage schedule
means here, and the edge and regularizer terms are 1, since their internal coefficients
already carry their scale. The parser also accepts ten weight flags, none of which reaches
the loss. Two are printed at start up, which is the only thing that made any of them look active, and
the release notes list all ten. Two quantities
are selected per backbone on validation, the dead zone $d$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:loss_fid} and the
gate of Equation~\eqref{eq:gate}. The grid crosses the two gate rules with dead zones of 0.3, 0.6 and
0.9 dB and the tissue-bounded gate with radii of 41 and 61 pixels, everything else held at one value,
each setting trained at three seeds and scored on validation. It chose the tissue-bounded gate at
radius 41 with a dead zone of 0.6 dB for NAFNet and the percentile gate at 0.9 dB for the other three.
A setting is admissible only if it clears its bounds at its worst seed, and among those we take the
largest lower confidence bound on the weakest clinical measure, relaxing the bounds one step at a
time when none clears them. NAFNet and SwinIR were admitted at the strictest level. No setting
cleared any level for DnCNN or KBNet, so their reported setting is the least bad of the grid rather
than one that met a stated bound, and they are the two backbones with a measure that falls on test.
The supplementary file gives the bound and the four levels. Every source of randomness is seeded, up to the nondeterminism
of the convolution kernels, and the checkpoints chosen on validation are the ones scored on test.

\emph{Initialization and every constant.} \label{sec:constants}Every constant appears with its value where it acts and has one of four origins. Fixed structural
choices, never tuned, are the sharpness $\kappa = 10$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:soft_failure}, the
steepness 20 of Equation~\eqref{eq:gate}, the window sizes, the quarter resolution of the gain
network, the bound $\pm 0.08$ of the edge branch, the thresholds $\tau_i$ of
Table~\ref{tab:predicates}, taken from the clinical literature, and the budgets $B$ and $C$. Set once
on validation while the method was built and never revisited are $\alpha_{\max} = 0.15$, the gain
bound $\pm 0.20$, the gate percentile 0.15, the three tolerances of Section~\ref{sec:safety}, the
half strength background blend and the learning rates. Selected in the grid of
Section~\ref{sec:weight_selection}, on the validation split only, are the dead zone $d$ and the gate
rule. Learned by the objective are $\beta$, $\theta_1$ to $\theta_4$, $\tau_0$, $\beta_0$ and the
scale of the edge branch. The fifth rule weight $\theta_5$ is declared learnable and is not,
for the reason Section~\ref{sec:gradient_audit} gives.

The wrapper is not fitted from scratch. Each run restores the whole wrapper, the rule layer
included, from the corrector of the submitted version for that backbone and trains it for ten epochs
with a fresh optimizer state. Default layer initialization therefore describes those earlier models,
not the runs reported here. Nothing was ever chosen on the test split.


\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\emph{Data.} PKU37~\cite{geng2022triplet} provides 37 cross sections of $640 \times 640$ pixels from a
spectral domain scanner, references averaged over 50 frames, split by clean image identity into 27, 5
and 5 to give 1388, 173 and 173 pairs. It is the only training domain.
Duke17~\cite{fang2012sparsity} provides 17 paired B-scans of $970 \times 520$ from another
institution, of which we use the 16 the released loader reads, and Duke2013~\cite{fang2013fast} the
18 volumes of $900 \times 450$ of its test split. Both Duke sets come from one manufacturer and the 2013 evaluation reuses some 2012 material, so they
are two external datasets and not two independent scanners.

\emph{Transfer protocol.} Every model is trained once on PKU37 and applied to the Duke sets directly,
with nothing fitted, recalibrated or selected on Duke data beyond the per image quantity the gate of
Equation~\eqref{eq:gate} computes by the same rule as on PKU37. The original submission instead
adapted the wrapper for fifteen further epochs on the other subjects of the target set, leave one
subject out, keeping in each fold the epoch that scores best on the subject it then reports. Those
numbers are selected on the data they describe, so the supplementary file keeps them only as a
historical diagnostic.

\emph{Measures.} Peak signal to noise ratio against the clean reference measures fidelity. Six
clinically motivated measures, given as percentage change from backbone to corrected output, are the contrast to
noise ratio of tissue against background, the tissue contrast index for dynamic range in tissue, the
edge preservation index for agreement with the reference edge map, boundary sharpness for the
gradient across layer boundaries, the equivalent number of looks for speckle suppression, and the
signal to noise ratio against the noise floor. Each is computed per image then averaged, never pooled. The
173 pairs come from five clean image identities, so every count below describes repeated acquisitions
of five anatomies and not a population.

\subsection{Which properties and which constants matter}
\label{sec:predicate_ablation}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
One property at a time is switched off at test time, by setting its score to the satisfied value and
its failure map to zero, and the test set is re evaluated. Only $P_5$, $P_1$ and $P_2$ move any
measure by more than a quarter point, costing 3.19, 1.72 and 0.74 points of the speckle gain, while
the other three change nothing by more than 0.23, each removed on its own. We keep all six, because
they are the vocabulary of the rule layer and of the explanation shown to the reader, and one that
rarely fires here can fire on another scanner.

We then vary the base level and the two rule weights that carry the allocation, and replace the
Lukasiewicz conjunction by the product and by the minimum. The budgets are tied by the identity of
Section~\ref{sec:allocation_defect} and stay fixed. None of the fourteen variations turns a measure
negative, contrast staying between 3.5 and 4.5 percent and fidelity between 0.16 and 0.23 dB. Only
the base level has a visible effect, lowering it raising contrast and costing fidelity together,
while the two weights move the result by under a fifth of a point. The result therefore rests on the
shape of the rule layer and not on the particular conjunction.

\subsection{What each component contributes, and how it compares}
\label{sec:component_ablation}
\label{sec:matched}
Table~\ref{tab:components} removes one component at a time from the selected NAFNet model. Replacing
the rule layer by a uniform allocation cuts the contrast gain from 3.7 to 0.8 percent and the tissue
contrast index from 2.4 to 0.4 while doubling the fidelity cost. The gain of this trained model
therefore depends on its allocation, which is not the same as isolating the rule design from the
capacity of the correctors, since that needs a second model trained from scratch in a matched
configuration. Removing the edge
branch turns both negative, removing the cooperation map costs about a third of the contrast gain,
and removing background smoothing leaves contrast and the two edge measures almost intact while the
speckle measure collapses from 23.3 to 5.8 percent.

The lower block spends a similar budget three other ways on the same backbone output. The first is
the same architecture, objective and ten epochs with the rule layer replaced by a uniform allocation
throughout training rather than only at test time. It was fitted before the gate search, so it
carries the percentile gate and not the tissue-bounded gate NAFNet was selected with, matched in
architecture and not in configuration. It reaches a larger contrast gain and a much larger tissue
contrast index for 0.42 dB against 0.17, and is worse on four of the six measures, so the two are not
ordered and this remains an exploratory comparison. The other two are classical operators tuned on
the validation split. Both reach a tissue contrast index far above anything the wrapper does and both
fail elsewhere, lowering edge preservation by about 3.5 percent and the speckle measure by about 36
percent while raising boundary sharpness by about 25 percent by sharpening noise. Adaptive
equalization also costs 4.4 dB.

\subsection{Does the cooperation map predict error}
\label{sec:confidence_check}
Called a confidence map in the original submission, this component was tested against the absolute
error of the backbone output over \NImages{} images, and it does not predict that error. The quantity
tested is the demand map $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{c}$, high where the wrapper is told to
act, so one tracking backbone error would give a positive rank correlation. The measured value is
\CalibRho{}, ordered the wrong way rather than merely uninformative, and removing the pixels it ranks
least reliable raises the remaining error to 1.88 times its starting value where an oracle ranking
reaches 0.07. The map is useful even so, since replacing it by a constant lowers the contrast gain
from $+3.71$ to $+2.51$ percent and worsens five of the six measures. Nothing in the objective shows
the head the error of the backbone, so we call it a cooperation map and not a confidence.

\subsection{Do the theory and the rule layer hold up on real data}
\label{sec:theory_check}
\label{sec:gradient_audit}
\emph{Which parameters actually train.} A varying allocation map does not by itself show the rule weights were learned, since fixed
coefficients on varying inputs look the same. We ran one training step on the selected model and read
the gradient of every rule layer parameter. The base level $\beta$ and the weights of rules one to
four carry a gradient, and their values in the four checkpoints differ from what they were restored
with, by as much as 1.49 in $\beta$. Rule five is the exception. Its weight has no gradient path and
holds its initial value in all four, because the conflict it tests for needs more than one corrector
proposing at a pixel, which does not arise here. The thresholds $\tau_i$ likewise have no gradient
path, which is why they hold their literature values. Rule five therefore contributes nothing, so the
exact constant over the truth values the method produces is the four rule sum, \LipTheory{}, while
the five rule sum of Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} over all of $[0,1]^5$ gives 0.6014. We report the
first, since it describes the model that produced every number here.

\emph{Whether the conditions hold.} We perturb the rule truth values and compare the ratio of
Equation~\eqref{eq:stability_def} with the constant of Equation~\eqref{eq:lipschitz}, and measure the
tissue gain, the background leakage and the ratio of background standard deviations against the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cnr}. The bound holds with room to spare, \LipTheory{} against a
largest measured ratio of \LipMeasured{}. The constant is exact, so the gap is not slack in the proof
but a statement that real perturbations never reach the worst case. The allocation map spans \AllocMin{} to \AllocMax{} with mean \AllocMean{}, and
\AllocClamp{} percent of pixels sit at either end of its clamp, direct evidence that the rule layer
does work after the repair of Section~\ref{sec:allocation_defect}.

The margin condition holds on \CondMargin{} of \NImages{} images and the ratio condition on
\CondSigma{}, and both together on \CondBoth{}. Those \CondBoth{} are the images on which the two
conditions we measure hold jointly, not a count on which the whole theorem applies, since its other
hypotheses are properties of the decomposition and not quantities we evaluate per image. Where a
hypothesis fails the theorem predicts nothing, and the contrast change there does not differ
systematically in sign, so the conditions are sufficient and not necessary. Both leakage terms sit
far below the mean absolute change over the image, \GlobalChange{}, at \EtaT{} in tissue and \EtaB{}
in background, with the per image sign the direction the decomposition assumes on \EtaTPos{} and
\EtaBNeg{} of \NImages{}.

\subsection{Safety, and whether the correction invents structure}
\label{sec:safety_check}
A correction that raises contrast could sharpen a boundary that is not there or erase a faint one
that is, and the clean reference lets us measure both. Two measures move against it. An edge appears
where the reference has none on \EdgeInvCorr{} percent of pixels after correction against
\EdgeInvBack{} percent for the backbone, a rise on \EdgeInvUp{} of \NImages{} images, and the
retention of structures in the lowest decile of reference contrast falls from \WeakRetBack{} to
\WeakRetCorr{} percent, on \WeakRetDown{}. Both are significant under a two sided Wilcoxon signed
rank test on the paired per image values, at $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-12}$ and
$p = 6.2 \times 10^{-30}$. Those tests describe the 173 acquisitions and not 173 independent
anatomies, since the acquisitions come from five clean image identities, so we read them as
descriptive of this set rather than as evidence across patients. The size of the second effect is
what matters, a fall of 2.9 points of retention, about 7.2 percent of where it starts. Neither is zero by construction, since the clamp bounds how far a pixel
may move and not how many cross an edge threshold, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} lists both. What
separates the wrapper from a sharpening filter is Table~\ref{tab:components}, where the classical
sharpeners lose edge preservation and the speckle measure while it gains on both.

Three checks come out in its favor. The two layer boundaries barely move, the inner limiting
membrane shift falling from \IlmShiftBack{} to \IlmShiftCorr{} pixels and the retinal pigment
epithelium shift by less than a signed rank test separates from zero, which is an absence of measured
movement and not a demonstration that anatomy is preserved. Dark fluid like regions occupy
\DarkFrac{} percent of the image with a mean absolute change of \DarkChange{} inside them against
\GlobalChange{} overall, the largest anywhere being \GlobalMax{}, so the correction is quieter where
a dark region carries the finding. Boundary sharpness is not worsened on \BoundOK{} of
\NImages{} images. Of the three constraints of Section~\ref{sec:safety}, energy holds
on \ConEnergy{} and bounded change on \ConLip{}, and only the no sacrifice constraint ever fails, on
\NImages{} minus \ConPareto{}. None falls below two, so the candidate is returned in full everywhere
and the layer never acted on this test set.

\subsection{Cost of moving to a new backbone}
\label{sec:transfer_cost}
A new backbone means refitting the whole wrapper, not only its head. The trainable count is
\num{168630} shared parameters plus a head whose width follows the feature tensor it reads,
\num{4659} for KBNet, \num{7263} for NAFNet, \num{85908} for SwinIR and \num{234273} for DnCNN, where
the first convolution is wide. Against frozen backbones of \num{7583521}, \num{7009881},
\num{6939289} and \num{7029013} parameters that is 2.3, 2.5, 3.7 and 5.8 percent, so the shared part
is constant and the cost of attaching it follows how wide a tensor the backbone exposes. Every backbone is fitted on the
same 1388 pairs for the same ten epochs, so only the wall clock and the seed spread vary. One seed
takes 2.0 minutes for NAFNet and 8.3 for SwinIR on a single RTX 5070 Ti, following the cost of one
backbone forward pass, and the seed spread of the fidelity cost runs from 0.02 dB for NAFNet to 0.21
for DnCNN. The backbone with the widest spread is the only one whose clinical measure falls.
