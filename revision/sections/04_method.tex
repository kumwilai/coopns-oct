\subsection{Overview}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the method as six stages, taken in order here and in the two
sections that follow, two to a section. Stage one asks where the backbone output can be left alone,
two what change is available, three what is wrong and where, four where it should be applied, five
how, and six whether it is allowed at all.

\subsection{Stage one, the backbone and the cooperation map}
\label{sec:confidence}
The backbone is frozen after training and gives $\mathbf{b} = f_\theta(\mathbf{y})$. The cooperation
map $\mathbf{c}$ says where $\mathbf{b}$ can be left alone and where a change is wanted. A two layer
network $h_\phi$ reads a feature tensor $\mathbf{z}$ from inside the backbone and produces it,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c} = \sigma\!\left(\frac{h_\phi(\mathbf{z}) + \beta_0}{\tau_0}\right),
\label{eq:confidence}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $\tau_0 > 0$ is a learnable temperature and $\beta_0$ a
learnable offset. The complementary demand map is $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{1} - \mathbf{c}$.

Only this head depends on the backbone, since architectures expose feature tensors of different
width. We read the first encoder stage of NAFNet and KBNet, the first convolution of DnCNN and the
shallow feature convolution of SwinIR, and the head is a pair of convolutions sized to whichever
tensor it is given, the widths listed in the supplementary file. Everything after this stage is
shared, which is the precise sense in which the method is modular rather than architecture free.

\subsection{Stage two, the two correctors}
\label{sec:correctors}
Two changes of deliberately different kinds are proposed.

The \emph{gain} network proposes a multiplicative change. It runs at quarter resolution and emits a
brightness channel through a rectifier, so it can only brighten, and a contrast channel through a
hyperbolic tangent, so it can go either way. Their sum is scaled by $\alpha_{\max} = 0.15$ to give
the raw gain $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$. A multiplicative form suits speckle, which is itself
multiplicative~\cite{goodman2007speckle}, so bright tissue receives a larger absolute change than
dark background for the same relative adjustment.

The \emph{edge} network proposes an additive change $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathrm{e}}$ from the
intensity normalised residual, the edge magnitude of the backbone output, that output itself and an
unsharp mask. It is bounded to $\pm 0.08$ and scaled by a learned factor. An additive form suits
boundaries, which should be sharpened by the same amount in a bright layer as in a dark one.

A third small network predicts a per-pixel strength map $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ with values in $[0,1]$
from the failure maps of Stage three. It modulates how much of the raw gain survives at each pixel.
