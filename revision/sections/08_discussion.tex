\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why fidelity falls, and when that is the right trade}
\label{sec:psnr_tradeoff}
Fidelity falls in the mean on all four backbones, from 0.17 dB on SwinIR to 0.98 dB on DnCNN, and
the reason is structural. A denoiser trained on squared error already sits near a local optimum of
that error, so a change that raises contrast or sharpens a boundary generally moves away from it.
It is not universal image by image. The NAFNet panel of
Fig.~\ref{fig:subjective_pku37} gains 0.02 dB while its contrast rises, so what we report is an
observed average and not a law, an average cost under one decibel for the quantities a grader
reads.

Where the gain lands follows what each backbone leaves undone rather than how good it is overall. On
boundary sharpness NAFNet starts furthest below the clean reference and gains most, while DnCNN
starts above it and is the only one that falls. On edge preservation KBNet starts highest and is the
only one left unchanged. SwinIR is the instructive case, since windowed attention smooths hard inside
a window, leaving the most room on contrast and paying the least fidelity. A deployment that cannot
afford the payment lowers the gain bound $\alpha_{\max}$, though only bypassing the wrapper returns
the backbone output exactly.

\subsection{Limitations}
The correction is bounded by construction, so a backbone that destroyed a structure cannot be made to
recover it. Two safety measures move against it, the rate at which an edge appears where the reference has none
and the retention of the weakest structures, both in Section~\ref{sec:safety_check}. Neither cost is
zero, and the criterion that chose the operating point scores six measures and fidelity and sees
neither. We measured that blindness on validation, so the test split is spent once. The runner up
setting for NAFNet keeps more weak structure, 43.9 percent against 37.7, and invents fewer edges, so
a user who values faint detail above contrast should score these two during selection. We did not
reselect, since the criterion was fixed before the test split was scored. The measures are
established proxies and no clinician read the images, so Section~\ref{sec:safety_check} is
quantitative evidence and not clinical validation. The external sets are small, sixteen and eighteen
images, so the transfer results show behavior across scanners rather than a precise estimate for any
one device.
