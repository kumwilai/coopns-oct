\section{Training}
\label{sec:training}

This section gives the training procedure in full. It states what data is used, what each loss term
measures, how the parameters are initialised, how every constant was chosen, and what is left fixed.

\subsection{What data is used, and where a clean image is needed}
\label{sec:paired_data}
This point matters for the credibility of the method, so we state it plainly. Training uses paired
data. Each training sample is a noisy scan together with a clean reference produced by averaging
many repeated acquisitions. The clean reference enters two of the five loss terms, namely the
fidelity term and the clinical term, and it also supervises the additive edge branch. Nothing in the
method pretends to be unsupervised.

Testing uses no clean image at any point. The six clinical properties of
Section~\ref{sec:predicates} are computed from the noisy input and the backbone output alone. That
is what the phrase ground truth free means in this paper. It refers to the inputs of the rule layer
at test time, not to the training procedure. The clean reference is used to score the results, as it
is in any denoising study, but it never reaches the correction path.

\subsection{The five loss terms}
\label{sec:loss_terms}
Let $\mathbf{x}$ be the clean reference, $\mathbf{b}$ the backbone output and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ the
wrapper output. The objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} has the following terms.

\textbf{Fidelity.} This term keeps the output near the reference. It combines the squared error with
the structural similarity index~\cite{wang2004image},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fid}} = \lVert \hat{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{x} \rVert_2^2
 + \big(1 - \mathrm{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{x})\big).
\label{eq:loss_fid}
\end{equation}
A dead zone is applied to the peak signal to noise ratio part of this term. No penalty is charged
while the drop with respect to the backbone stays below $d$ decibels. The purpose is to buy clinical
quality with a small and bounded amount of fidelity rather than to defend fidelity at any price.

\textbf{Clinical improvement.} This term rewards a rise in the clinical measures relative to the
backbone. Working with the logarithm of a ratio makes the term scale free, so an image with a low
starting contrast is not treated differently from an image with a high one,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clin}} = -\log\frac{\mathrm{CNR}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\mathrm{CNR}(\mathbf{b})}
 - \log\frac{\mathrm{TCI}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\mathrm{TCI}(\mathbf{b})}.
\label{eq:loss_clin}
\end{equation}
The implementation adds the same construction for the edge preservation index, the equivalent number
of looks and the signal to noise ratio, each with its own weight, so that a gain in one measure
cannot be bought by a collapse in another.

\textbf{Property calibration.} The rule layer compares each property score with a threshold. If the
scores drift away from the thresholds the comparison becomes uninformative. This term pulls the
scores toward the operating point,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} = \sum_{i=1}^{6}\big(s_i(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - \tau_i\big)^2 .
\label{eq:loss_pred}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Cooperation.} The wrapper should act where the backbone is unsure and stay quiet where it is
confident. We measure the agreement between the uncertainty map and the map of estimated correction
potential with the Pearson correlation $\rho$, and penalise disagreement,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coop}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\big(1 - \rho(\mathbf{u}, \boldsymbol{\pi})\big).
\label{eq:loss_coop}
\end{equation}
The Pearson correlation is used because it is differentiable everywhere, it is invariant to an
affine change of either map, and it introduces no window size to tune.

\textbf{Edge supervision.} The additive branch is trained to reproduce the gradient that the backbone
lost, measured with the Sobel operator $\nabla$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{edge}} = \big\lVert \nabla\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \nabla\mathbf{x} \big\rVert_2^2 .
\label{eq:loss_edge}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Optimisation schedule}
The backbone is trained first on the training split with the squared error alone and is then frozen.
Every result in this paper uses that frozen backbone without further change.

The wrapper is then trained in two stages. In the first stage only the clinical terms are active, so
the correctors learn to move the image in a useful direction. In the second stage the fidelity term
is switched on, so the correctors learn how far they may go. The switch happens at the midpoint of
the schedule. We use AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019decoupled} with decoupled weight decay of $10^{-4}$,
random crops of $96 \times 96$ pixels, and a batch of sixteen crops, reduced to eight for the two backbones with the largest memory footprint. Three learning rates are used, a
lower one for the corrector networks and higher ones for the strength predictor and the rule layer,
because the last two have far fewer parameters and therefore need larger steps to move at all.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:training} states the procedure in full. The supplementary file repeats it with
every constant filled in.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Training the wrapper around a frozen backbone}
\label{alg:training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require frozen backbone $f$, paired set $\{(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})\}$, epochs $E$, dead zone $d$, seed
\State seed every generator, then initialise the wrapper parameters $\phi$
\State set each property threshold $\tau_i$ to its value from the clinical literature
\State freeze $f$, and hold its normalisation statistics fixed for the whole run
\For{epoch $=1$ to $E$}
  \For{each batch of $96 \times 96$ crops}
    \State $\mathbf{b} \gets f(\mathbf{y})$ \Comment{no update reaches $f$}
    \State $\mathbf{u} \gets$ confidence map from the features of $f$
    \State $s_i, \mathbf{m}_i \gets$ score and failure map of property $i$, for $i=1..6$
    \State $\boldsymbol{\pi} \gets$ correction potential, and the gain and edge proposals
    \State $\mathbf{a} \gets$ allocation from the rule layer applied to $s_1..s_6$
    \State $\mathbf{g} \gets$ gate from the background rule, evaluated on $\mathbf{b}$
    \State $\hat{\mathbf{x}} \gets \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{g} \odot \mathbf{a} \odot (\text{proposals})$
    \If{epoch $\le E/2$}
      \State $\mathcal{L} \gets \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clin}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}
             + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coop}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{edge}}$
    \Else
      \State $\mathcal{L} \gets \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clin}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}
             + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coop}} + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{edge}}
             + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fid}}(d)$
    \EndIf
    \State update $\phi$ with AdamW, three learning rate groups
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State \Return $\phi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The clinical terms act alone in the first half of the schedule so that the correctors learn a useful
direction before they are told how far they may go. The fidelity term with its dead zone $d$ enters
in the second half and sets that distance. Holding the normalisation statistics of $f$ fixed matters
more than it appears. One of the four backbones contains batch normalisation, and leaving those
layers in training mode lets the reference against which every clinical change is measured drift
while the wrapper is being fitted to it.

\subsection{How the operating point was chosen}
\label{sec:weight_selection}
The terms of Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} carry fixed relative weights. Only one quantity in the
objective is tuned, the fidelity dead zone $d$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:loss_fid}, together with one
structural choice, the rule that decides where a correction is allowed to act. Everything else is
held at the value stated in Table~\ref{tab:constants} for every backbone and every run. We state this
plainly because a search over many weights would be hard to check and easy to overfit, and because
one budget is enough. The dead zone is exactly the quantity a reader wants to control. It says how
much fidelity the method is permitted to spend, and the rest of the objective then decides how to
spend it.

The selection runs on the validation split alone. For each backbone we train every setting at three
random seeds and score all of them on the validation images. A setting is admissible only if it
clears its bounds at its \emph{worst} seed rather than on average, so a setting cannot qualify by
being fortunate once. Among the admissible settings we take the one with the largest lower confidence
bound on its weakest clinical measure, which prefers a setting that is reliably good over one that is
occasionally excellent. When no setting clears the first pair of bounds we relax them one step at a
time and record which step was needed, so a backbone that required a looser condition is visible in
the table rather than hidden by it.

Two properties of this procedure matter more than the rule itself. The first is that the three seeds
are fixed and every source of randomness is seeded, so a setting can be trained again and give the
same model. On a graphics processor that holds up to the nondeterminism of the convolution kernels
themselves, which we do not force to a deterministic choice because the cost is large. Two runs of
one seed therefore agree closely rather than exactly, and the seed to seed spread we report is an
upper bound on what a repeat would show. The second is that the checkpoints chosen on validation are the checkpoints scored on the
test split. Nothing is retrained between selection and reporting. An earlier version of this work
selected a setting on validation and then trained a fresh model with it, and because that training
was not seeded the model in the tables was never the model that had been selected. The spread between
two runs of one setting was larger than the spread between settings, which made the choice close to
arbitrary and let a backbone pass on validation and fail on test. Reporting the selected weights
directly removes that failure, and the seed to seed spread is now reported beside every number so a
reader can see how much of a difference is real.

\subsection{Initialisation and the choice of every constant}
\label{sec:constants}
Table~\ref{tab:constants} lists the constants that carry a design decision, where each acts, and how it was chosen. The remaining constants, namely the window sizes, the gate steepness, the crop size and the batch size, are structural and are listed in the supplementary file.
We separate three cases. A constant marked \emph{measured} was set from a property of the data. A
constant marked \emph{selected} was chosen on the validation split and never on the test split. A
constant marked \emph{fixed} is a structural choice that was not tuned. Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}
reports how much the results move when the constants of the rule layer are changed, which is the
direct test of whether any of them is doing hidden work.

The networks use the default initialisation of their layers. Two exceptions are deliberate. The bias
of the brightness head starts at a small positive value so that the multiplicative branch begins from
a mild brightening rather than from zero, which avoids a flat region of the loss at the start of
training. The threshold of each property starts at the value reported in the clinical literature for
that property and is then free to move.
