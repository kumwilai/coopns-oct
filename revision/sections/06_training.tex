\section{Training}
\label{sec:training}

\subsection{The six loss terms}
\label{sec:loss_terms}
Let $\mathbf{x}$ be the clean reference, $\mathbf{b}$ the backbone output and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ the
wrapper output. The objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} has the following terms.

\textbf{Fidelity.} Let $\Delta = \mathrm{PSNR}(\mathbf{b}) - \mathrm{PSNR}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ be the
drop against the backbone in decibels and $\Delta_s$ the corresponding drop in the structural
similarity index~\cite{wang2004image}. Neither is charged until it exceeds a dead zone,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fid}} = 5\,[\Delta - d]_+ + 15\,[\Delta - d - 0.4]_+^2
 + 20\,[\Delta_s - 0.005]_+ ,
\label{eq:loss_fid}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_+$ is the positive part. The term is flat while the drop stays below $d$ decibels,
linear for the next four tenths of a decibel and quadratic beyond that, so a small loss of fidelity
is free and a large one is expensive. The dead zone $d$ is one of the two quantities selected per
backbone in Section~\ref{sec:weight_selection}.

\textbf{Clinical improvement.} This term charges the wrapper only when a measure falls short of a
target taken from the backbone, so a gain in one measure cannot be bought by a collapse in another.
Let $q_m$ be the value of quantity $m$ on the wrapper output and $t_m = \kappa_m q_m(\mathbf{b})$ the
target, the backbone value of the same quantity scaled by a fixed factor. Writing
$\varepsilon_m = t_m - q_m$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{clin}} = \frac{1}{\sum_m w_m} \sum_{m \in M} w_m
 \Big( 3[\varepsilon_m]_+ + [\varepsilon_m]_+^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} s_m \varepsilon_m^2 \Big).
\label{eq:loss_clin}
\end{equation}
The set $M$ holds nine quantities, the three preservation scores of local contrast, boundary gradient
and texture, then EPI, CNR, BS, ENL, SNR and the ratio of the backbone squared error to the wrapper
squared error. Their weights $w_m$ are 1, 5, 1, 15, 40, 5, 8, 40 and 10 and their factors $\kappa_m$
are 1.12, 1.12, 1.00, 1.05, 1.20, 1.12, 1.10, 1.15 and 0.87, the last permitting about six tenths of a
decibel. The indicator $s_m$ is one for ENL, SNR and the squared error ratio, a symmetric pull that
holds those three near target instead of letting them run. None of the nine is used at test time.

\textbf{Preservation scores.} This term acts on four scores $g_j$ separate from the six properties of
Table~\ref{tab:predicates}, comparing the corrected output with the backbone output on local
contrast, the gradient across layer boundaries, the local variance carrying texture, and the gradient
magnitude at edges. With $\delta_j = g_j(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - g_j(\mathbf{b})$, the backbone value fixed,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} = \sum_{j=1}^{4} v_j \Big( 3[-\delta_j]_+ - 2.5[\delta_j]_+
 + 2\big[\tfrac{1}{2} - g_j(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\big]_+ \Big) ,
\label{eq:loss_pred}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_+$ is the positive part and $v_j$ is 3, 2, 2 and 1 in that order. A drop costs more
than a rise earns, and any score below one half is charged again. The thresholds $\tau_i$ of the six
properties play no part here. They enter Equation~\eqref{eq:soft_failure}, but the rule layer that
evaluates it runs without gradient tracking, so no path reaches them and all six hold their
Table~\ref{tab:predicates} values throughout training.

\textbf{Cooperation.} The wrapper should act where the backbone leaves room and stay quiet elsewhere.
We take the Pearson correlation $\rho$ between the demand map $\mathbf{u}$ and $\boldsymbol{\pi}$, the
summed proposal strength the rule layer reads, and penalise disagreement,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{coop}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\big(1 - \rho(\mathbf{u}, \boldsymbol{\pi})\big).
\label{eq:loss_coop}
\end{equation}
The Pearson correlation is differentiable everywhere, invariant to an affine change of either map, and
introduces no window size to tune.

\textbf{Edge supervision.} The additive branch is trained to reproduce the gradient that the backbone
lost, measured with the Sobel operator $\nabla$ and weighted toward the places where the clean
reference has an edge. Let $\mathbf{w}$ be the clean Sobel magnitude divided by its mean and clipped
at ten, $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ its square normalised again to unit mean, and $\bar{\mathbf{g}}$ a Sobel
magnitude divided by its own maximum. With $\delta = [1.05\,\mathrm{EPI}(\mathbf{b}) -
\mathrm{EPI}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})]_+$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{edge}} = \;&10\,\big\lVert \sqrt{\mathbf{w}} \odot
  (\nabla\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \nabla\mathbf{x}) \big\rVert_2^2 \\
 &+ 10\,\big\lVert \sqrt{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}} \odot
  (\bar{\mathbf{g}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - \bar{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x})) \big\rVert_2^2
 + 45\,(3\delta + \delta^2).
\end{split}
\label{eq:loss_edge}
\end{equation}
The first part is the gradient the correction must restore. The second compares normalised edge
magnitude, which is insensitive to overall brightness. The third refuses an edge preservation index
below five percent above the backbone.

\textbf{Bounds and background protection.} The last term collects the constants that keep the change
small, smooth and confined. Let $a$ be the mean absolute change over the upper half of the image, $e$
the mean absolute output of the additive edge branch, $\sigma_B$ the standard deviation over the lower
quarter, $\mu_T$ the mean over the upper half and $\mathbf{m}$ a soft mask on intensities below the
fifteenth percentile of the backbone output,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}} = \;&40[0.002 - a]_+ + 200[a - 0.006]_+^2
 + 2\,\overline{\lVert \nabla \boldsymbol{\alpha} \rVert} \\
 &+ 30[e - 0.06]_+^2
 + 80\big[\sigma_B(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) - 0.75\,\sigma_B(\mathbf{b})\big]_+ \\
 &+ 100\Big[\tfrac{\sigma_B(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\sigma_B(\mathbf{b})} - 0.95\Big]_+
 + 150\Big[0.99 - \tfrac{\mu_T(\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\mu_T(\mathbf{b})}\Big]_+ \\
 &+ 2\,\overline{\lvert \hat{\mathbf{x}} - \mathbf{b} \rvert \odot \mathbf{m}} ,
\end{split}
\label{eq:loss_reg}
\end{equation}
where a bar denotes the mean over pixels and the first two parts are clipped at five. The floor stops
the wrapper from learning to do nothing and the ceiling from overshooting fidelity, the third keeps the
allocation map smooth so that it leaves no Sobel trace of its own, the fourth bounds the edge branch,
and the last three hold the background noise and the tissue brightness, the denominator and the
numerator of the signal to noise ratio.

\subsection{Data and optimisation schedule}
\label{sec:paired_data}
Training uses paired data, a noisy scan and a clean reference averaged over repeated acquisitions,
and the reference enters the fidelity and clinical terms and supervises the edge branch. Testing uses
no clean image at any point, since the six properties read only the noisy input and the backbone
output, and the reference only scores the results. The backbone is trained first, on the training split with the squared error alone, and is then
frozen. The wrapper is then trained in two stages. Only the clinical terms are active at first, so
the correctors learn a useful direction, and the fidelity term switches on at the midpoint, so they
learn how far they may go. Both stages use AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$ on $96 \times 96$ crops
in batches of sixteen, eight for DnCNN and SwinIR, for ten epochs in all, with base learning rates of
$2 \times 10^{-4}$ for the corrector networks, $5 \times 10^{-4}$ for the cooperation head and the
strength predictor and $3 \times 10^{-4}$ for the rule layer, and the output layers of the correctors
and the background smoother at two to eight times the corrector rate. The supplementary file restates
the procedure as pseudocode.

Throughout both stages the normalisation statistics of $f$ are held fixed. DnCNN is the one backbone with batch normalisation, and left in training mode 45 of its
buffers move during fitting, so the frozen reference against which every clinical change of that
backbone is measured reads 30.68 dB and a boundary sharpness of 1.031 when the statistics are held
fixed against 31.21 dB and 0.948 when they are not.

\subsection{How the operating point was chosen}
\label{sec:weight_selection}
The terms of Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} carry fixed relative weights, the same for every backbone
and seed. The clinical term dominates at 6, the property term is 1, the cooperation term is 0.2, and
the fidelity term is 0 in the first stage and 1 in the second, which is what the two stage schedule
means here, and the edge and regulariser terms are 1, since their internal coefficients
already carry their scale. The parser also accepts ten weight flags, for the clinical
term, the two contrast measures, fidelity in two forms, the edge term, three per measure
preservations and efficiency. Not one of them reaches the loss. Two are printed at start up, which is
the only thing that made any of them look active, and the release notes list all ten. Two quantities
are selected per backbone on validation, the dead zone $d$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:loss_fid} and the
gate of Equation~\eqref{eq:gate}. The grid crosses the two gate rules with dead zones of 0.3, 0.6 and
0.9 dB and the tissue-bounded gate with radii of 41 and 61 pixels. It chose the tissue-bounded gate
at radius 41 with a dead zone of 0.6 dB for NAFNet and the percentile gate at 0.9 dB for the other
three. Everything else is held at one value throughout. Each setting is trained at three seeds and
scored on validation. A setting is admissible only if it clears its bounds at its
\emph{worst} seed rather than on average, and among the
admissible settings we take the one with the largest lower confidence bound on its weakest clinical
measure. When no setting clears the first pair of bounds we relax them one step at a time and record
the step that was needed, so a backbone that required a looser condition is visible rather than
hidden. Every source of randomness is seeded, up to the nondeterminism of the convolution kernels, and
the checkpoints chosen on validation are the checkpoints scored on test.

\subsection{Initialisation and the choice of every constant}
\label{sec:constants}
Every constant appears with its value where it acts and has one of four origins. Fixed structural
choices, never tuned, are the sharpness $\kappa = 10$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:soft_failure}, the steepness 20 of Equation~\eqref{eq:gate}, the window sizes, the
quarter resolution of the gain network, the nominal bound $\pm 0.08$ of the edge branch, the
thresholds $\tau_i$ of Table~\ref{tab:predicates}, taken from the clinical literature and unchanged by
training to four decimals in every run, and the budgets
$B$ and $C$, tied by the identity of Section~\ref{sec:allocation_defect}. Set once on the validation
split while the method was built and never revisited are $\alpha_{\max} = 0.15$, the gain bound
$\pm 0.20$, the gate percentile 0.15, the three tolerances of Section~\ref{sec:safety}, the half
strength background blend and the learning rates. Selected in the grid of
Section~\ref{sec:weight_selection}, on the validation split only, are the dead zone $d$ and the gate
rule. Learned by the objective are $\beta$, $\theta_1$ to $\theta_5$, $\tau_0$, $\beta_0$ and the
scale of the edge branch. Every layer uses its default initialisation with one deliberate exception,
the bias of the brightness channel of the gain network starts at 0.5, so the raw gain begins at
$0.5\,\alpha_{\max}$ in flat tissue rather than on a flat region of the loss. Nothing was ever chosen
on the test split, and Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} reports how much the results move when the
constants of the rule layer are changed, the direct test of whether any of them materially affects the result.
