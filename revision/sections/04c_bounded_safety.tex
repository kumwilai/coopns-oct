\section{Bounded Correction and Majority Based Acceptance}
\label{sec:bounded_safety}

\subsection{Stage five, applying the change}
\label{sec:composition}
The admitted gain is the raw proposal modulated by the allocation and the strength map, then bounded,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\boldsymbol{\Delta} \odot \mathbf{a} \odot \boldsymbol{\lambda},\, -0.20,\, 0.20\big).
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
A gate suppresses any change in the dark background,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g} = \sigma\big(20\,(\mathbf{b} - q_{0.15})\big),
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $q_{0.15}$ is the fifteenth percentile of the backbone output of that image, a percentile
rather than a fixed level so the gate works across scanners of different brightness. The second form, used where the
selection of Section~\ref{sec:weight_selection} chose it, replaces the percentile by the per image
Otsu threshold and dilates the result with a maximum filter of side $R$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g} = \mathrm{maxpool}_{R}\big(\sigma(20\,(\mathbf{b} - \mathrm{otsu}(\mathbf{b})))\big),
\quad R = 41 ,
\label{eq:gate_halo}
\end{equation}
which widens the admitted region to a band around tissue, because the edge branch earns its contrast
and texture gains just outside the tissue boundary. Both gates read the detached backbone output and
carry no gradient. Neither is ever exactly zero, since a logistic is strictly positive and a maximum
filter preserves that. Over the test scans the smallest value Equation~\eqref{eq:gate_halo} reaches
is 0.017 to 0.071 by image and Equation~\eqref{eq:gate} 0.045 to 0.20, so the darkest background is
attenuated by one to two orders of magnitude and not excluded, and the background measures of
Section~\ref{sec:safety_check} are evidence rather than a property the gate guarantees.

The gated gain is applied multiplicatively to give the change $\mathbf{v}$, with the brightening part
smoothed before it is added, which attenuates high frequency variation in that contribution,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v} = \big(\,\mathrm{smooth}(\mathrm{ReLU}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) \odot \mathbf{b})
 + (\boldsymbol{\alpha} - \mathrm{ReLU}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})) \odot \mathbf{b}\,\big) \odot \mathbf{g},
\label{eq:apply}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{smooth}$ is a three by three mean filter, so a brightening is spread over a
neighborhood while a darkening acts where it was proposed. The background is then smoothed. Writing
$\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{v}$ for the image at that point,
$\boldsymbol{\beta} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{erode}_5(\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{g})$ for the blend weight
and $S(\mathbf{u})$ for a three by three mean taken over the masked pixels only, the smoothed image
is $\tilde{\mathbf{u}} = (\mathbf{1} - \boldsymbol{\beta}) \odot \mathbf{u} + \boldsymbol{\beta} \odot S(\mathbf{u})$.
The erosion keeps the blend away from the tissue border, and since $\mathbf{g}$ is never one the
weight is small rather than zero inside tissue. The edge proposal is added after this, and the result
is clipped,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q} = \mathrm{clamp}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{u}} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\mathrm{e}} \odot \mathbf{g},\, 0,\, 1\big).
\label{eq:candidate}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage six, the safety decision}
\label{sec:safety}
The candidate $\mathbf{q}$ is not returned directly. Three constraints are checked on it.

\textbf{Aggregate quality must not fall.} Define for any image $\mathbf{t}$ the energy
\begin{equation}
E(\mathbf{t}) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{6} s_i(\mathbf{t})
 + 0.1 \sum_{i=1}^{6} \max\big(0,\, 0.5 - s_i(\mathbf{t})\big),
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
which is small when the image is good and whose second term penalizes any property below one half,
so a good average cannot hide one broken property. The constraint is
$E(\mathbf{q}) \le E(\mathbf{b}) + 0.10$.

\textbf{No property may be sacrificed for another.} The second constraint requires that at least one
property improves by more than $0.01$ and that no property falls by more than $0.10$.

\textbf{The image must not move far.} The third constraint requires
$\lVert \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{b} \rVert_2 \,/\, \lVert \mathbf{b} \rVert_2 < 0.2$.

Let $n \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ be the number of constraints that hold. The output is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{x}} = (1 - w)\,\mathbf{b} + w\,\mathbf{q},
\qquad
w = \begin{cases}
1, & n \ge 2, \\[2pt]
0.05 + 0.10\,\dfrac{n}{3}, & n < 2 .
\end{cases}
\label{eq:blend}
\end{equation}
The candidate is accepted in full whenever any two constraints hold, so one violation does not by
itself withhold the change. Below two the output falls back to the backbone while keeping five
percent of the change when none holds and about eight when one does. This is a majority test on three
measured conditions and not an enforcement of three bounds, and Section~\ref{sec:safety_check}
reports how often each holds.

\subsection{Sufficient conditions for non decreasing contrast}
\label{sec:cnr_guarantee}
Write
$\mathrm{CNR}(\mathbf{t}) = (\mu_\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{t}) - \mu_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{t})) / \sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{t})$
for the separation between tissue and background divided by the variability of the background.

\begin{theorem}[Contrast margin]
\label{thm:cnr}
Let $\eta_\mathcal{T} = \mu_\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{q}) - \mu_\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{b}) \ge 0$ be the gain in
tissue mean and let $\eta_\mathcal{B} \ge \mu_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{q}) - \mu_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b})$
bound the leakage into the background. Suppose the tissue and background sets are the same for
$\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{q}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, that
$\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) > 0$ and $\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b}) > 0$, so both
ratios are defined, that the correction does not raise the background variability, so
$\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{q}) \le \sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b})$, and that the bounded numerator
$\big(\mu_\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{b}) - \mu_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b})\big) + w\,(\eta_\mathcal{T} -
\eta_\mathcal{B})$ is non negative, which holds whenever the backbone already separates tissue from
background and $\eta_\mathcal{T} \ge \eta_\mathcal{B}$. Then the output of
Equation~\eqref{eq:blend} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CNR}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \;\ge\;
\frac{\big(\mu_\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{b}) - \mu_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b})\big) + w\,(\eta_\mathcal{T} - \eta_\mathcal{B})}
     {\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b})} .
\label{eq:cnr_bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, whenever $\eta_\mathcal{T} > \eta_\mathcal{B}$ the correction cannot reduce the
contrast to noise ratio.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The mean over a fixed pixel set is linear, so
$\mu_\mathcal{T}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) = \mu_\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{b}) + w\,\eta_\mathcal{T}$ and
$\mu_\mathcal{B}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \le \mu_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b}) + w\,\eta_\mathcal{B}$, which bounds
the numerator of $\mathrm{CNR}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ below by the numerator of
Equation~\eqref{eq:cnr_bound}. On the background $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ is the same convex combination
pixel by pixel, so Minkowski's inequality on the centered values gives
$\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) \le (1-w)\,\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b}) + w\,\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{q}) \le \sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{b})$.
Dividing the two bounds gives the claim, and $w > 0$ in every branch of Equation~\eqref{eq:blend}.
\end{proof}


Three parts of the design encourage these hypotheses rather than enforce them. The gate of
Equation~\eqref{eq:gate} keeps $\eta_\mathcal{B}$ small by suppressing change in the dark region, the
brightness branch cannot be negative, and the background smoothing lowers
$\sigma_\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{q})$ rather than raising it. None settles the matter, since the net tissue change also passes
through the contrast branch, the edge branch and a final clipping, any of which can turn it negative.
Section~\ref{sec:theory_check} therefore measures each hypothesis rather than assuming it.

Both guarantees are deliberately narrow. Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} bounds how far the routing
decision can move, not whether it is correct. Theorem~\ref{thm:cnr} concerns contrast alone. Neither
promises that the output is closer to the clean reference, which Section~\ref{sec:results_id} shows
it usually is not. They concern the wrapper, not the denoiser underneath it, and
Section~\ref{sec:theory_check} tests both on real images.
