\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{O}{ptical} coherence tomography (OCT) is the standard non invasive way to image the
retina~\cite{huang1991optical}. Every OCT image carries speckle, a multiplicative disturbance that
arises from coherent light scattering inside tissue~\cite{goodman2007speckle}. Speckle blurs the
border between retinal layers, lowers the contrast between tissue and background, and makes
automatic layer segmentation less reliable~\cite{devalla2018drunet}.

Deep denoisers remove much of that disturbance, but they are almost always trained to minimize the
squared error against a clean reference, so a denoiser can raise the peak signal to noise ratio (PSNR)
of a scan while making it less useful to a clinician. Layer boundaries lose definition, the contrast
between tissue and the dark vitreous falls, and thin structures fade into the background, none of
which a PSNR number shows.

\subsection{Motivation}
The gap we address is not a missing denoiser. It is that nothing sits between the denoiser and the
clinical reader to state what a good OCT image should look like and to repair the ways a denoised one
falls short. Another neural block would hide the reason for every change inside learned weights,
while a clinician, a regulator or a reviewer who sees a modified scan needs to know which property
was judged deficient, where, and how large a change was allowed. We therefore treat post-correction
as its own problem, keeping the denoiser frozen and attaching a small wrapper written as named image
properties, explicit rules over them and explicit limits on the size of any change. Every learned map
in the decision path is bounded, and its effect reaches the image only through a named rule.

\subsection{New ideas in this paper}
\label{sec:contributions}
The new ideas are four. Edge filters, gating maps and fuzzy operators are not among them.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Six clinical properties that need no clean image.}
Section~\ref{sec:predicates} defines six properties computed from the noisy input and the denoiser
output alone, so they run on any scanner at test time. Each returns a score for the image and a map
of where it fails, both as formulas.

\item \textbf{A rule layer whose decision cannot jump.}
Section~\ref{sec:negotiator} builds the allocation from five rules joined by a conjunction with
bounded slope and proves that a shift in the evidence moves the decision by at most a constant the
rule layer itself fixes, and Section~\ref{sec:theory_check} measures the response the images
actually produce against it.

\item \textbf{A safety layer that decides how much of a proposal to keep.}
Section~\ref{sec:safety} states three constraints at three scales, and how many hold sets the
fraction of the change applied. Section~\ref{sec:cnr_guarantee} proves a margin condition under which
contrast cannot fall, tested on every test image.

\item \textbf{One design for four denoisers.}
Only the head that reads backbone features changes with the backbone, while the rules, budgets,
thresholds and loss weights do not, and Section~\ref{sec:results_ood} carries the fitted wrapper to
two other datasets with nothing refitted.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\emph{Denoising of OCT images.} Learned denoisers fall into three families, residual convolutional
networks such as DnCNN~\cite{zhang2017beyond}, designs that gate or reweight channels such as
NAFNet~\cite{chen2022simple} and KBNet~\cite{zhang2023kbnet}, and transformers such as
SwinIR~\cite{liang2021swinir}. Methods built for OCT address the scarcity of paired data directly,
through triplet cross fusion~\cite{geng2022triplet} and sparse representation~\cite{fang2013fast},
although the earlier of those still needs one high quality scan of the same eye~\cite{fang2012sparsity}.
All are trained to reduce a pixel error, so the quantity they optimize is not the quantity a
clinician reads, and work on quality assessment for OCT~\cite{wang2019quality} measures clinical
quality but does not act on it. This paper closes that loop.

\emph{Confidence and uncertainty.} Deep ensembles and their
relatives~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple} are the standard way to attach a confidence to a
prediction, while in OCT the networks that segment layers~\cite{devalla2018drunet} report an accuracy
rather than an uncertainty, so a reader learns how often the model is right and never where it is
unsure. We feed such a map into the correction decision, and Section~\ref{sec:confidence_check}
reports that the map we built is not a confidence in this sense.

\emph{Symbolic reasoning and expert networks.} Neuro symbolic computing~\cite{garcez2023neurosymbolic} couples a learned model with explicit rules,
and differentiable fuzzy logic makes the coupling trainable because the connectives become smooth
functions of continuous truth values~\cite{vankrieken2022analyzing}. In medical imaging it has generated spinal reports from a symbolic graph of
domain knowledge~\cite{han2021unifying} and predicted patient deterioration with a probabilistic
logic program over convolutional features~\cite{fadja2022neural}. Fuzzy membership functions have
been embedded in networks that process OCT directly, guiding the segmentation of fluid
lesions~\cite{chen2023deep} and replacing learned feature extraction so that what the model attends
to reads as a logical statement~\cite{kim2025pixel}.

A parallel line builds an image model from cooperating experts. Steered mixtures of experts combine
constant experts through soft gating kernels with an explicit steering term~\cite{ozkan2023steered},
also for OCT speckle removal~\cite{ozkan2024denoising}, and restoration networks route an input
through experts of different capacity by its difficulty~\cite{zamfir2025complexity}. Outside imaging,
evolving fuzzy models identify actuator dynamics~\cite{precup2021evolving}, learned models join a
physical forward operator in ultrasound tomography~\cite{fan2022model}, and sensitivity to initial
conditions is treated as a source of uncertainty in simulation~\cite{casquilho2025effect}. All rest
on the principle we rely on, that a learned component is easier to trust when part of the decision is
written in a language a person can read. What is missing is a rule layer that acts on the output of a
strong denoiser, names the properties it protects and limits how far it may move the image.
