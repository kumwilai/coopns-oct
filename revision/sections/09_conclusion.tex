\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a post correction layer for OCT denoising that is attached to a frozen denoiser rather
than replacing it. It states six clinical properties in closed form, turns them into a per pixel
decision through five named rules, and limits how far the resulting change may move the image, so a
change can be traced back to the property that motivated it and the constraint that allowed it to
stand. Across four backbone families the same rules with the same fixed weights raise the clinical
measures at a fidelity cost between 0.17 and 0.98 dB, and no measure falls on three of the four. The
exception is boundary sharpness on DnCNN, which falls by 1.4 percent from a value that already
exceeded the clean reference, showing where the method has no room to act. The measures are proxies, and the next step is a reader study asking
whether the changes the method makes are the changes a grader would want.
