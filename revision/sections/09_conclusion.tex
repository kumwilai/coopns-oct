\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a post correction layer for OCT denoising that is attached to a frozen denoiser rather
than replacing it. The method states six clinical properties in closed form, turns them into a per
pixel decision through five named rules, and limits how far the resulting change may move the image.
Each of those three parts is written down rather than learned in the dark, so a change to an image
can be traced back to the property that motivated it, the place where that property failed, and the
constraint that allowed the change to stand.

The decision layer is stable in an exact sense. Its response to a change in the evidence is bounded
by a constant that can be computed from the trained weights, and that constant is attained rather
than conservative. Under three stated conditions the correction cannot reduce contrast to noise
ratio, and we measure how often those conditions hold rather than assuming them.

Across four backbone families and three datasets from three sources the same correction policy
improves the clinical measures at a small and bounded cost in fidelity, with only the confidence head
refitted per backbone. The remaining work is clinical rather than technical. The measures used here
are proxies, and the natural next step is a reader study that asks whether the changes the method
makes are the changes a grader would want.
